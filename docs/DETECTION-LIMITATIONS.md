# What Data Scope can and cannot tell you

Data Scope reports what it found and how sure it is. Absence of a finding is never a statement that a file is clean, and an access result is never stronger than the data behind it. This page lists the limits customers should know before relying on the results. Share it with anyone who reads the reports.

## Sensitive data

**Every detection is a possible match.** Each finding carries a rule id, a confidence (High, Medium or Low) and masked evidence. A regular expression hit is not a confirmed record of personal or health information; a person decides that.

| Rule | Looks for | Known limits |
|---|---|---|
| DS-SSN-01 US Social Security number | `NNN-NN-NNNN` or `NNN NN NNNN` that pass the Social Security Administration's range checks; unformatted numbers next to "SSN", "SS#", "social security" or "TIN"; and every value in a spreadsheet or export column whose header names an SSN, including numbers Excel stored without their leading zero | Formatted look-alikes without a keyword (part numbers) are reported at Medium. Numbers starting with 000, 666 or 9 are never SSNs: those starting with 9 are reported as ITINs by DS-ITIN-01 when they are in the IRS ranges, and otherwise not at all. Unformatted numbers with no keyword or SSN header are not reported. A quoted CSV cell containing a comma shifts that row's columns, so the header rule misses it. Numbers issued after randomisation (2011) can't be told from look-alikes by format. |
| DS-ITIN-01 US Individual Taxpayer ID number | `9NN-NN-NNNN` or `9NN NN NNNN` whose middle two digits are in the IRS ranges (50-65, 70-88, 90-92, 94-99), with the same keyword and column rules as SSNs (ITINs are often kept in an SSN column) | Formatted look-alikes without a keyword are reported at Medium. Made-up numbers starting with 9 outside those ranges (900-12-3456) and the advertising numbers 987-65-4320 to 4329 are not reported. |
| DS-PCI-01 Payment card number | 13 to 19 digits with a known issuer prefix that pass the Luhn checksum | Test card numbers (4111 1111 1111 1111) are reported, by design. One in ten random digit strings passes Luhn, so context decides the confidence. |
| DS-BANK-01 Bank routing and account | A checksum-valid ABA routing number near an account number | US only. Other countries' formats besides IBANs (DS-BANK-02) are not detected. |
| DS-BANK-02 IBAN | A country code, check digits and account, the right length for that country, passing the ISO 13616 check; High next to "IBAN", "bank", "account" and similar words | Only IBANs written in capitals, together or in groups of four. About 1 in 97 random strings of the right shape pass the check, so part numbers in that exact shape can be reported at Medium. |
| DS-DL-01 Driver's licence | A licence number right after "driver's license", "DL#", "DLN" and similar labels (Medium), or in a column whose header names a licence (High) | Numbers with no label or header are not reported: they have no checksum and look like any other id. State ID cards are not detected. Values that are one digit repeated or a run such as 12345678 are skipped. |
| DS-PASS-01 Passport | 6 to 9 digits after up to 2 letters, right after "passport" (Medium) or in a passport column (High) | Numbers with no label or header are not reported. Passport formats that mix letters among the digits (some European ones) are missed. |
| DS-PII-01 Name with address or date of birth | A labelled name followed by a US street address or date of birth; a name above an address with a state and ZIP (a mailing label); rows of a table with name and address or date of birth columns. High for 5 or more people, but never Restricted on its own | US street addresses only. Unlabelled names in running text are not found. A business's name not ending in a word such as Inc, LLC or Corp, next to its address, is reported as a person. Names in lower case or in other alphabets are missed. |
| DS-CRED-01 Private key | PEM private key blocks | Keys in binary formats (PFX, keystore files) are not read. |
| DS-CRED-02 Password or secret | `password=`, `pwd:`, connection-string passwords, AWS access key ids | Many false positives in documentation and sample configs; most secret formats are not covered. |
| DS-PHI-01 Health information | Two or more clinical terms or ICD-10 codes, higher next to a personal identifier | Keyword based: misses health information written in plain language, and flags medical reference material. For example "hypertension (I10), taking lisinopril" is not found: ICD-10 codes are counted only with their decimal part (E11.9), and conditions and medicine names are not in the term list (the test pack's `patient-intake.txt` shows it). |
| DS-DOB-01 Date of birth | A date next to "DOB" or "date of birth" | Dates without that label are not reported. |

Not detected in V1: names or addresses on their own, licence and passport numbers without a label or column header, state ID cards, non-US national ids, email address lists (unless an administrator adds the custom starter rule for them), and anything in a language other than English.

**Custom rules (D133)** are whatever an administrator wrote: Data Scope checks that a rule is safe to run, not that it is right. A pattern finds only what it describes; a match is at most 256 characters; "only near these words" looks 80 characters either side; a file is reported once it has the rule's minimum number of *different* values (a value repeated counts once, and at most 10,000 values are counted per rule). Starters such as IP addresses also match version numbers and other look-alikes unless near words are added. A change to the rules makes the next scan read every file again. Custom matches count towards a compliance standard only when an administrator ticks "Custom rule" for it, and only at Label Confidential or Label Restricted.

## Compliance standards

- **Tags, not verdicts.** A file is tagged with a standard (HIPAA, PCI DSS, GLBA, US state breach laws, Passwords and keys) when it has a possible match, at the confidence the administrator chose, in a kind of data that standard counts. A tag means "this file may hold data this standard covers", not that it does, and no tag does not mean a file holds none.
- **What each standard covers is wider than what Data Scope detects.** Each standard lists its gaps on the Compliance standards screen, for example medical record numbers, card verification codes on their own, state ID card numbers, biometric data and anything in scanned images. Files not read are shown apart and never counted.
- **Reviews win.** A file a reviewer labelled Public or Internal is not tagged, even with matches.

## Files that are not read

These are counted as **not read**, shown on the Sensitive data screen and in every report, and never treated as clean.

- **Read by contents, not names:** every file is opened and read as whatever its first bytes show it is: text and data files (CSV, JSON, XML, logs, scripts and configuration read as text, never run), Word, Excel and PowerPoint (2007 and later), PDF, saved emails (.eml, with their attachments) and zip files (the files inside). So `customers.dat`, a file with no extension and a spreadsheet renamed `photo.jpg` are read.
- **Never opened, by name:** programs, disk images, video, audio and fonts (.exe, .dll, .msi, .iso, .vhdx, .mp4, .mp3, .ttf and similar). A document renamed to one of these names is not found.
- **Types not read:** Word 95 and Excel 95 and older, gzip, xz, tar and other archives besides zip, 7-Zip and RAR, icons, zips in the ZIP64 format (very large archives), databases, and any other binary format. Each is named in the file's note where Data Scope recognises it.
- **Inside zips and emails:** each file is read with the same reader and limits as on a share, including files with unknown names and images (attached or shown in an email); programs and media inside them are not opened. Password-protected files inside are counted but never opened. Zips and emails inside each other are read 3 deep. A zip or email that was only partly read says how many of its files were read and why the rest were not.
- **Text in images and scanned PDF pages** is read by OCR (Tesseract, English, D129), and the file's note says so with the average confidence. OCR misreads some characters: a stray space or a wrong digit hides a number. Pages that are sideways, upside down or tilted up to 15 degrees are turned and levelled first (D131); pages at steeper angles, such as a photo of a page lying on a desk, pages with text running several ways, and short text in capitals and digits only that reads poorly both ways up may still be misread. It does not read handwriting, very small or faint text, or languages other than English reliably. JBIG2 and JPEG 2000 pictures in PDFs, and WebP or HEIC images on servers without those Windows decoders, are not decoded. Pictures share 20 seconds per file: a long scanned PDF may be read only for its first pages (Partial). Images over 50 million pixels are not read (Too large). When OCR is turned off or can't run, images are Not read and are tried again on every scan.
- **Encrypted or password-protected files:** reported as Encrypted.
- **Too large:** Office files over 50 MB, PDFs over 25 MB or 200 pages, zips over 100 MB (or expanding to more than 256 MB), emails over 50 MB. Only the first 1,000,000 characters of any file are kept (reported as Partial), except Outlook mailboxes, which have their own budget.
- **Damaged files, timeouts (30 seconds per file) and reader errors:** reported as Unreadable or Timed out. Files are read in a separate reader process: a file that crashes it or runs it out of memory twice is reported as Unreadable, and the scan carries on.
- **Offline and cloud-only files** (OneDrive Files On-Demand, Azure File Sync and similar): never opened, so nothing is downloaded; reported as Offline.
- **Access denied:** files and folders the scan account cannot read are reported as gaps.
- **Excluded by an administrator:** folders and file types an administrator left out on **Scan scope & exclusions** are not read (D124). They are reported as not read or as coverage gaps, never as clean, and their sensitivity is Unknown. Files already found under a folder that is no longer scanned stay as last seen.
- **Older Office files, Outlook messages, 7-Zip and RAR (D132):** the text of Word, Excel and PowerPoint 97-2003 files is read, but not the pictures in them (no OCR), and Excel formulas are read as their last saved result, never recalculated. Outlook .msg files are read like saved emails, without addresses. Only the part of a 7-Zip or RAR archive split into several files that is on the share is read. Password-protected files, and archives whose file list is encrypted, are counted, never opened.
- **Outlook data files (.pst, .ost, D134):** read message by message: subjects, bodies, attachments of the types Data Scope reads and attached messages, but not sender and recipient addresses, contact fields other than notes, or links to files elsewhere. A mailbox keeps up to 5,000,000 characters of text (20,000,000 in full scans) and stops reading at two thirds of the file's time limit, so a large mailbox is Partial and its note says how many messages and attachments were read; a full scan reads more of it. Findings are for the mailbox as a whole: the screen does not say which message or folder a match is in. A .pst or .ost inside a zip, an email or another mailbox is not opened. A mailbox's text is scanned in pieces of 1,000,000 characters, so a custom rule's minimum number of different values is counted within a piece, and a number cut by a piece boundary with no blank line nearby can be missed.
- **Embedded content:** pictures pasted into Word, Excel and PowerPoint files are read by OCR, up to 50 per file, largest first (D131); vector drawings (EMF, WMF), the text in charts, and embedded objects such as a workbook inside a document are not read; macros are never run.

All of these limits are settings (`Scanner:Content`), and every file that hit one says which.

## Stale and duplicate data

- **Stale** is judged from the modified and created times the scan records, not Windows' last-access time. A file people only open, never change, looks stale unless file activity is collected for its server and recorded someone opening it within the activity retention (365 days by default).
- **Times can be wrong:** copying a file with some tools keeps the old modified time but sets a new created time, so the copy is not stale; restores and migrations can reset both.
- **Duplicates** are likely copies: the same name and size in more than one place. Contents are not compared, so two different files can match, and renamed or edited copies are not found. Files under 1 KB and hidden or system files are left out.

## Who has access

- **Effective access is computed, not asked of Windows.** Data Scope evaluates the stored share and NTFS permissions with the stored directory snapshot the way Windows does (deny first, inheritance, the share permission limiting NTFS). It is checked against Windows itself in lab check W21; until that is recorded, treat differences as possible.
- **Results are ranges with certainty.** When something needed is missing, the answer says "possibly" or "up to" instead of a single right: share permissions or local groups the scan account cannot read (needs local administrator on the file server), members from another forest or unresolved SIDs, groups past the directory sync's object limit, and folders whose permissions could not be read.
- **Not modelled in V1:** Dynamic Access Control and central access policies, claims, file-level permissions that differ from their folder (permissions are read per folder, so answers are for files that inherit), CREATOR OWNER entries (noted, not evaluated), Owner Rights, other domains and forests, `SeBackupPrivilege` and other privileges, local accounts on file servers other than local groups, and group membership changes since the last directory sync.
- **"Anyone in the domain"** means every enabled domain user through Everyone, Authenticated Users, Domain Users or a group they are all in.
- **Reading** means the computed access includes reading file contents. A write-only entry, as on a drop box folder, is not counted as reading (D125).
- **Exposure map:** people counts are enabled accounts in the latest directory sync; disabled accounts with permissions are listed apart. A group's count is everyone it lets read, so a person reached through several groups counts under each. Folders whose permissions could not be read show no people. Only the 20,000 folders with the most Restricted files are evaluated on very large estates (the screen says when).

## Activity

Access on the **Who has access** screens means "could open", from permissions. What people actually did comes only from the file servers' Security logs, on the **File activity** screen, read from each file server's Security log (D102), and each event says how much it proves (D97):

- **Recorded** (4663, 4660, 4670, 1102): Windows recorded the operation. For 4663 the operation comes from the rights used on the handle; Windows logs a right once per handle, not every byte read or written, so "Read" means the file was opened and read from, not how much.
- A client computer that already has a file open or cached through the share can read or write it again without asking the file server, so repeat use from the same computer can leave no new event.
- **Request** (4656): someone asked Windows for a handle with those rights. Nothing proves the handle was used.
- **Access check only** (5145): access through a share was checked. This is **not** proof that the file was opened or read, is hidden by default and is never counted as a read.
- **Share connection** (5140): someone connected to a share.
- **Denied** means Windows refused.
- Windows only logs what the file server's audit policy and each folder's audit settings (SACLs) ask for. A folder without auditing has no events, and no events never means nobody opened it. Data Scope does not change audit policy; `Enable-DataScopeAuditing.ps1 -Check` shows what is in effect.
- With the script's defaults, reading a folder's list of files is not audited (only refused attempts are), and successful reads are audited for files only. With `-NoReads`, successful reads are not seen at all.
- Events that leave the Security log before Data Scope reads them (the log wrapped, was cleared, or started again) are lost. Data Scope records each such gap with its record range and says so on the screen, but cannot say what the missing events were, or whether they were about files at all. A server that is off or unreachable loses nothing as long as its log still holds the events when Data Scope gets back to it.
- A delete (4660) has no path of its own; it is matched to the earlier event on the same handle, logon session and process (D100). If none is found the delete is shown without a path.
- Paths are matched to shares by each share's folder on the server, learned from share events or set by an admin (D99). Events in folders that match no share are counted and shown as unmatched.
- "File" or "folder" comes from the latest scan. Something created and deleted between scans shows as unknown ("Read or list folder").
- Alerts (D105) are patterns worth a look, not proof of wrongdoing: a planned clean-up or a permissions project raises the same alerts as an attack. They come only from collected events, after each collection pass, so activity on a server that is off, or in events that were missed, raises no alert (missed events raise their own). Thresholds are per person per server; deletes spread across several accounts or below the threshold raise nothing.
- Unusual behaviour (D166) compares a person only with their own past on the same server, learned from the last 28 days. Someone new, someone who changed role, or someone who was off for weeks looks unusual; someone who was already copying data while Data Scope learned looks normal. Nobody is judged before five active days, and only what collection saw counts: folders without auditing, a server that was off and events that were missed are invisible. "Touched" includes share access checks, which show a file was reached, not read. Hours are the Data Scope server's local time, so people in other time zones may look like they work odd hours; a person active in 20 or more hours of the day is not judged on hours. Sensitive folders are judged from the last scan's ratings of each top-level folder.
- The person is who Windows recorded. Through a service or an application running as a service account, that is the account, not the person behind it.

## Scope

- Data Scope only sees what it scanned and when: every report states the scan time, the share coverage and the gaps.
- Last-access times are never used: Windows usually doesn't keep them, and reading files can change them.
- Rescans read only new and changed files (by size and modified time) unless the classification policy changed. A file changed without either changing is not reread until the next policy change.
